Our Studio Guide to Success…

Dance Class Expectations

Communication

The Peace River Scottish Society primarily uses email to communicate club information to dancers and families. We also have this webpage and social media accounts for news and events. Instructors communicate primarily by text message. Please keep your contact information up to date with both instructors and the PRSS board.

What Will My Dancer Need?

A filled water bottle, dance shoes, and a positive, happy attitude to ensure a fun, successful class.

Dress Code

Girls: Body suit or tight-fitting t-shirt/tank top, tight shorts or skirt above the knees, knee-high socks, Highland ghillies or ballet slippers (Wee Tots/new dancers only).

Boys: Tight-fitting t-shirt or tank top, shorts, knee-high socks, Highland ghillies or ballet slippers (Wee Tots/new dancers only).

Hair: Tied back during class — braid, ponytail, or bun.

Regular Groups / Classes

Classes are held weekly, with groups formed by skill level, exam level, and age. Dancers may occasionally be moved between groups, always with notice and a discussion with the dancer and parent first.

Competitive / Technique / Choreography Classes

These extra classes run through the year and require the skill level and dedication to succeed. There's an extra cost associated — reach out to discuss if your dancer is interested.

Class Cancellation

Classes may occasionally be cancelled due to unforeseen circumstances; texts, emails, and social media posts will go out to parents in advance. Missed classes are made up when possible. Don't assume no dance because school buses were cancelled — wait for confirmation.

Sickness

Our studio is small and germs carry quickly. Please keep sick dancers home out of respect for classmates and teachers, and let Carey or Averi know if your dancer will miss class.

Washroom Breaks

Our program shares space with Fitness on the Go. Please have dancers use the washroom before class starts. Younger classes have a student teacher to help with bathroom breaks.

Attendance

Attendance is mandatory — new techniques, skills, and recital/exam preparation happen in every class. Attendance is taken. Dancers who miss 4+ classes risk being removed from special choreography or being unprepared for certain exams unless they catch up on their own time. Private catch-up classes are an additional cost, not included in the set fee.

Practice

Dancers are encouraged to practice at home — repetition builds muscle memory and musicality. Reach out to instructors if you're unsure how to support practice at home.

Parents

Classes are held at Fitness on the Go, a separate business from our club. Please don't gather in the boot room/gym entrance during class — only at drop-off and pick-up. Parents are welcome at the studio door when it's open, but please don't come in and out during class unless it's an emergency.

Special Events Throughout the Year…

St. Andrews Night

An informal fall showcase — a potluck supper organized by the PRSS Board, generally held at the end of November. All enrolled dancers perform.

Dance Outs

Community events, trade shows, culture days, festivals, and visits to seniors' residences and schools to showcase and promote the club. Great low-pressure practice for performance and confidence.

Annual Recital

Held each spring; organized by the PRSS Board (parent help requested) with the program created by instructors. Extra classes, dress rehearsals, and costume fittings fill the weeks before. There's a small ticket fee for spectators to cover show expenses.

SDTA Medal Test Examinations

Medal testing = Highland Dance Exams. Non-competitive; dancers as young as 3 show their skills to a Scottish examiner and get written feedback, collecting medals from Scotland as they progress.

Medal Test Benefits

A non-competitive way to progress, get feedback from a different set of eyes, and build a real sense of accomplishment moving up through the grades.

About the Scottish Dance Teachers Alliance

Our club is part of the Scottish Dance Teachers Alliance (SDTA) — both Carey and Averi hold their teaching certificates through the SDTA. Other recognized associations include the British Association of Teachers of Dance (BATD) and the United Kingdom Alliance (UKA), each with their own exams and syllabus.

How It Works

Peace River is part of the Winter SDTA exam tour — the examiner typically flies in from Scotland and tours through Alberta and sometimes BC and Saskatchewan, usually in March (exact date varies). Exams can fall on a school day; lower testing levels can expect 30–90 minutes. The examiner stays in Peace River 1–2 days; results and evaluation sheets are distributed to dancers shortly after.

Types of Exams

Dance Stars (ages 3–6, 6 levels), Highland Exams (Preparatory through Scottish Award 1/2/3), National Exams, Jig & Hornpipe Exams, Theory Exams, and Professional Exams (teaching exams, from age 16). Choreography exams are offered less often.

Exam Results

Dance Stars: Pass. Highland/National/Jig/Hornpipe: Pass, Pass Plus, Commended, Commended Plus, Highly Commended. Theory: Pass, Merit, Honors — Honors can lead to a nomination for the SDTA Scholarship Event.

Exam fees are set by SDTA and cover the examiner's tour and physical awards. Fees aren't due until mid-February.
